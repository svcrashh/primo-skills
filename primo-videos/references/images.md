# Generated images

The videos are made of the product's own screens and the brand's own material. **A generated image is the exception**:
`scripts/images.mjs` makes one through the OpenAI API only when a scene needs something that exists nowhere else. Each
one is shown on the board as a still, marked as generated by AI and with its cost, and goes into the video only after
the person's ok. Without a key, or without the ok, the video goes on without it.

## When: the order

1. **The product's real screen** (a recording or a capture): whenever the scene is about the product
   (`references/brand.md`, "Product screens"; `references/product.md`).
2. **The brand's own photos, illustrations and icons**: the kit (`references/kit.md`), what the brand reading found
   (`references/brand.md`), what the person sends.
3. **A generated image**: only when the scene needs something that is in neither 1 nor 2: a setting, an object, a scene
   photo, a backdrop or a texture. For example: the desk where the engine sets the real recording on a laptop with a
   blank screen; a paper, fabric or concrete texture behind the text; the pile of paper invoices that stands for "before".

Before generating, ask yourself whether a plain background, a brand shape or a typographic scene would say the same.
Usually it does, and costs nothing.

## Never generate

- **The product's screen or interface**, or any part of it (a dashboard "like ours", a phone with the app open): a
  screen is never invented. It is recorded, captured or rebuilt from the product's tokens (`references/brand.md`). A
  device with a blank or dark screen, as a setting, is fine: the real screen goes over it in the engine.
- **The product itself** in any form (a physical product, its packaging, a "new version"): a made-up product is a
  promise nobody made.
- **A logo** or any brand mark, the person's or anyone's: the logo is the brand's file, animated by the engine.
- **A real person, a customer or a testimonial**: no face presented as someone who exists or who used the product.
- **Text inside the image** (words, numbers, labels, signs, a chart): the text belongs to the engine, in the brand's
  font. The prompt says so, and a picture that came out with text anyway is redone.
- **"Photos of the brand"**: `photo-mosaic`, and every style that asks for the brand's own photos, take only real,
  approved photos (`references/catalog/choosing.md`). A generated picture does not stand in for them.

## Shown before it is used

- Each generated image is shown on the board as a still **marked as generated, with its cost** (the
  `generated: { "model", "usd" }` field of the option, piece or scene) before it goes anywhere.
- Only after the person's ok does it go into a scene; that scene's still then goes through "Approve still" like any
  other (`recipes/board.md`).
- **Without a key** (exit code 3) **or without the ok**: do not insist. Ask the person for a photo of theirs, or pick a
  style that needs none (`kinetic-typography`, `counting-number`, `drawn-explainer`…), and say so in one line.

## Cost: cheap options, then the chosen one in detail

| Step | Quality | What it costs |
|---|---|---|
| The options of a scene (2 to 4, in one call with `--n`) | `low` | ~US$ 0.006 each at 1024x1536 (paid: US$ 0.0061); ~US$ 0.007 at the 16:9 size |
| The one the person chose, redone (`final`) | `high`, with the chosen option as the reference | paid: US$ 0.043 at 1024x1536 without a reference, ~US$ 0.055 at 2048x1152, up to US$ 0.08 with three references (each reference adds ~US$ 0.012) |

- **The cap:** US$ 0.50 of images per video (`scripts/costs.mjs`; the video's `costs.json` changes it). Every call is
  estimated and checked against what was already spent **before** the API is called. Over the cap, exit code 5 and the
  message say what to ask: whether to raise it (`node $SKILL/scripts/costs.mjs cap <project> --image-usd <n>`).
  Raising it is the person's decision, never yours.
- A scene with three options and one final costs about US$ 0.10, so the default cap covers about four generated scenes.
- What gets noted is the **real** cost, from the tokens the API answers with, one line per call in
  `<project>/costs.jsonl` (`node $SKILL/scripts/costs.mjs show <project>`). The estimate follows the measured numbers
  at 1024x1536; at the 2048-wide sizes it comes out above what is paid (2048x1152 in `high`: ~US$ 0.063 estimated,
  ~US$ 0.055 paid), which is the safe side for the cap.
- **The final is a new picture**: close to the chosen option, not identical to it. It goes back to the board for
  approval. If the person liked the option better, the option stays (it is already in the folder and already paid);
  another `final` costs the same again.

## The commands

```sh
node $SKILL/scripts/images.mjs estimate --format 16x9 --quality low --n 3 --project <video>   # free: the cost, against the cap
node $SKILL/scripts/images.mjs generate --project <video> --name scene-02 --n 3 --format 16x9 --prompt @prompts/scene-02.txt
node $SKILL/scripts/images.mjs final    --project <video> media/generated/scene-02-b.png [--add "a little more light from the left"]
node $SKILL/scripts/images.mjs generate --project <video> --name scene-03 --ref media/generated/scene-02-final.png --prompt "…"
node $SKILL/scripts/images.mjs batch    --project <video> batch.json [--parallel 4]
node $SKILL/scripts/images.mjs board    --project <video>        # the `generated` of each image, for board.json
node $SKILL/scripts/images.mjs models                            # free: the image models the account sees; checks the key
```

- `--project` (the video folder) is required for every paid command: the cost goes to its notebook and counts against
  its cap. The files go to `<project>/media/generated/` (`--out` changes it; relative paths are from the project).
- `--prompt` takes the text or `@file.txt`. `--ref` (repeatable) edits from reference images instead of generating
  from nothing. `--quality` is `low` by default; `final` uses `high`. `--model` changes the model
  (default `gpt-image-2.5-sunburst`, the one the cost is calibrated for; `models` lists the others).
- Names: with `--n 1`, `<name>.png`; with more, `<name>-a.png`, `<name>-b.png`…, the same letters as the board's options.
  The same name again overwrites the files: a new round gets a new name (`scene-02-r2`).
- `batch.json` (paths relative to the project):
  ```json
  { "out": "media/generated", "format": "16x9", "quality": "low", "label": "textures",
    "common": "Palette, light, lens, style and the no-text line, added to every prompt.",
    "items": [ { "name": "paper", "prompt": "…" }, { "name": "linen", "prompt": "…", "n": 2, "refs": ["media/x.png"] } ] }
  ```
  The whole batch is checked against the cap before the first call. One item that fails does not stop the others
  (exit code 1 at the end); no key or the cap stops all of them.

| Exit code | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | done | show the images on the board |
| 2 | usage (a flag, a missing file, a bad batch) | fix the command |
| 3 | OpenAI cannot be used now: no key, the key was refused, or the account has no credit | go on without generated images (above) |
| 5 | over the video's image cap; nothing was called | ask the person whether to raise the cap |
| 1 | anything else (the API failed, the safety filter refused the prompt) | read the message; a refusal means rewriting the prompt, and it is not charged |

**The key:** `OPENAI_API_KEY` in the environment, or the line `OPENAI_API_KEY=sk-…` in `~/.claude/secrets/openai.env`.
The person writes it there themselves, never in the chat; the script never prints it. `OPENAI_API_BASE` swaps the API
address (the tests point it at a local fake).

## images.json

Beside the images, one entry per image:

| Field | What it is |
|---|---|
| `file`, `name` | the file (in this folder) and the name it was made under (`final` uses it) |
| `prompt`, `model`, `quality`, `size`, `format` | what was asked |
| `refs` | the reference images, relative to the project |
| `usd`, `costFrom` | what this image cost (the call's cost split among its images), and whether it came from the `tokens` or, when the API did not say, an `estimate` |
| `generated` | always `true` |
| `from` | in a final: the option it was redone from |

`images.mjs board --project <video>` prints `{ "media/generated/scene-02-a.png": { "model": "…", "usd": 0.0061 } }`,
exactly what the board takes in `generated`.

## Size and framing

The size follows the video's format (`--format`, `16x9` by default; `references/formats.md`): the short side 1152 px
and the long side at the frame's shape, always a little larger than the frame, so the engine only scales it down.

| Format | Frame | Size sent | In the frame |
|---|---|---|---|
| `16x9` | 1920×1080 | 2048x1152 | whole, at 94%, nothing cut |
| `19x9` | 2280×1080 | 2432x1152 | whole, at 94%, nothing cut |
| `21x9` | 2520×1080 | 2688x1152 | whole, at 94%, nothing cut |
| `9x16` | 1080×1920 | 1152x2048 | whole, at 94%, nothing cut |
| `4x5` | 1080×1350 | 1152x1440 | whole, at 94%, nothing cut |
| `1x1` | 1080×1080 | 1152x1152 | whole, at 94%, nothing cut |

- 2048x1152 and 1152x2048 were already used with this model; the others follow the same rule and were not called for
  real yet. If the API refuses one, use `--size 2048x1152` (or another accepted size) with `--format`: the script says
  what is cut (`1536x1024 in the 16x9 frame (1920x1080): only the middle 1536x864 shows, 160 px of height (top and
  bottom) cut, enlarged to 125%: it will look soft`).
- A video in two formats gets one image per format, each framed for its own (the same prompt and the approved one as
  `--ref`), never one image cropped into the other.
- The image is 1.07× the frame, so a slow push-in (`kenBurns` up to ~1.07) stays sharp; past that it softens.
- An image that fills only part of the frame (an object in a card, a side panel) gets `--size` in the shape of that box.

## Writing the prompt so it comes out in the brand

```
<the subject, concrete: what it is, what it is made of>, <where it is>.
Palette: <dominant color #hex> as the <background, walls, surface>, <accent #hex> only on <one small element>,
<neutral #hex> for the rest.
Light: <one source, its direction and warmth>, <the time of day>.
Framing: <lens and camera height>, the subject in the <right third>; the <left half> calm and empty, no detail there.
Style: <a natural photograph, not CGI, not a 3D render | a flat illustration with the brand's shapes>.
No text, no letters, no numbers, no logos, no signs, no screens with content, no people.
```

- **Palette with a role for each color**, from the brand reading, with the hex: the dominant one (background,
  surface), the accent (on one small element only), the neutrals. A list of colors without roles comes out as a rainbow.
- **One light**, said in words (direction, warmth, time of day), the same in every image of the video.
- **Room for the engine's text:** say which part of the frame stays calm and empty, where the scene's text will go
  (16:9: usually one side or the lower third; 9:16: inside the safe band, y 250–1450, `references/formats.md`). In the
  styles where text never crosses an image (`VIDEO.texto = 'fora-da-imagem'`), the text goes beside the image: then
  the image needs no empty area, but the layout does.
- **No text in the image**, said every time. Look at each picture anyway: a letter that came out means redoing it.
- Concrete nouns, materials and textures, not "beautiful" or "modern". No real brand, product, artist or person by name.

## A series that holds together

- In a batch, the palette, the light, the lens, the style and the no-text line go once, in `common`.
- The first approved image becomes the reference for the next ones: `--ref media/generated/<approved>.png` and
  "same light, same palette and same lens as the reference image; a different subject: …". Each reference adds
  ~US$ 0.012.
- The same model, size and quality across the series.
- The series is shown side by side on the board before any of it goes in.

## How a generated image gets into the video

1. **In the project.** `generate` already writes to `<project>/media/generated/`. An image made anywhere else is copied
   into `<project>/media/` first: the board serves only files from inside the project, and the engine and the templates
   read paths relative to it.
2. **Shown as options.** In `board.json`, a `choices[]` item of the scene with one option per image
   (`"image": "media/generated/scene-02-a.png"`, plus its `generated`). An element that is not the scene's look (a
   texture, an object) can go in `pieces[]` with `"kind": "Photo"` and its `generated`. `images.mjs board` prints the
   `generated` of every file.
3. **The pick.** On the board's `choice` event, run `final` on the picked option, `patch` the board with the final
   image and its `generated`, and wait for the ok on it.
4. **In the scene.**
   - A scene built on the engine: `imagem(parent, 'media/generated/scene-02-final.png', { width: px(W), height: px(H), objectFit: 'cover' })`,
     with `kenBurns` or `camera` for movement (`references/engine.md`, "Photos and recordings"); as a background, inside
     the scene's `camada`.
   - A template that takes a picture: the `rolling-list` `foto` panel (`src`), the `chat-bubbles` image card
     (`{ "tipo": "imagem", "src": … }`), the `drawn-explainer` `s.image(…)` (listed in `midia`). Each `TEMPLATE.md` says
     where its paths are relative to. Not `photo-mosaic` (the brand's own photos only), not the `light-trail-teaser`
     `imagem` fragment (a real screen only); the symbol shapes of `kinetic-typography` and `phone-3d` and the icons of
     `card-stack`, `card-bento`, `timeline` and `drawn-flow` come from the brand.
5. **The still.** Build the scene's still with the image in place and show it with the same `generated`; it goes through
   "Approve still" like any other.
6. **The person's own picture wins.** On `replace-still`, rebuild the still from their file; nothing is generated.
